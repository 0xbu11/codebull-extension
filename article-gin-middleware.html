How many times does my middleware chain advance per request?

2026-08-3011 min 40 s window / 600 requests / 4 probesgin v1.12.0

📊 The numbers here come from this run’s remote dashboard: gin-middleware-20260830

Four probes inside a running gin service, no code change, eleven minutes of watching — and the numbers answer for themselves.

Why these four places

r.Use(requestID, accessLog, auth) registers three layers — but that is a fact about startup. Once it is running, how many times does one request actually advance the chain? Nothing tells you. So I picked four places: the ServeHTTP entry, the route match in handleHTTPRequest, Context.Next(), and redirectTrailingSlash.

Standing inside my own handler would have been easiest, and it is the least useful spot: the requests that never reach a handler are exactly the ones I wanted to see.

Only one thing was predictable up front — if the chain is never cut short, the ratio should be ×3. What was not predictable: whether any request fails to finish the chain, and how many are dealt with inside the framework entirely.

What each signal says here

metric (how often) — hits and a per-bucket curve on every node. 600 requests taken, 1,800 advances, 120 redirects. The ×3 and ×0.2 are those three numbers divided, not estimated.

latency (how long) — on the entry interval only: p50 0.612ms, p95 54.581ms. It measures how long a request spends *inside the framework*, which is a different question from how long the handler takes.

p50 and p95 are nearly ninety times apart because the distribution is bimodal: of 600 requests, 82 land between 54 and 56 ms and the rest sit under 1 ms, with almost nothing in between. All 82 fall in the four phases that run the middleware chain — the fifth phase, the 301 redirect that returns before the chain starts, has none at all. The control round (the same 600 requests, entry probe only) has a p95 of 0.166 ms: this tail arrives with the extra probes, not with the traffic.

log (what) — the route node captures httpMethod and rPath, in four shapes: GET /health 240 times (two phases share that URL), and GET /nope, PUT /health, GET /health/ at 120 each. This column puts the origin of ×0.2 right on the surface — not a sampled estimate, but five phases of 120 requests, exactly one of which carries the trailing slash.

flamegraph (from where) — the dashboard’s Call stack tab holds the full frame chain taken to reach a node. Advance the chain has eight, exactly four depths × whether the request went through serveError:

handleHTTPRequest · Next                                    240
handleHTTPRequest · Next · requestID · Next                  240
handleHTTPRequest · Next · requestID · Next · accessLog · Next   240
handleHTTPRequest · Next · requestID · Next · accessLog · Next · auth · Next   120
serveError · Next                                           240
serveError · Next · requestID · Next                         240
serveError · Next · requestID · Next · accessLog · Next      240
serveError · Next · requestID · Next · accessLog · Next · auth · Next   240

One Next(), four stack depths — the shape of the chain, drawn. Nothing above it the first time, three layers stacked on the last.

The eight add up to 240×7 + 120 = 1,800, which is the advance count exactly. The odd 120 is the 401 phase: auth cut the chain, so the call that auth would have made downward never happened. The other seven are full.

What came out

1,800 ÷ 600 = 3, three on average. Meanwhile ×0.2 (120 requests) turned around on a 301 inside the framework before the chain even started. ×3 is a mean, not a per-request fact — the eight stacks above already spell out the difference.

The edge from Match the route to Advance the chain is labelled 0–4 unobserved, and it names them: requestID, accessLog, auth, serveError. The middleware chain is itself the unmeasured layer. The range is not noise — Next() is called at different depths, which is the same thing those four stacks already said.
